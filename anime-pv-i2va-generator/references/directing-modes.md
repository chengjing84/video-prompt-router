## Battle highlight

The goal is readable force, changes in advantage, speed contrast, and a clear result. High energy comes from understandable action, not from effect count.

Choose or derive a causal topology such as:

- probe → lose advantage → recognize the rule → counter → finish;
- lock-on → pursuit → terrain block → direction change → escape or capture;
- encirclement → exploit a gap → reposition → chain reaction → breakthrough;
- discover structure → bait an attack → environment responds → redirect force → result;
- anomalous rule → first failure → understanding → precise use → payoff;
- long pressure buildup → extremely brief exchange → delayed result.

For each key exchange, make this readable: anticipation → acceleration → contact point → material or energy response → force direction → displacement or state change → aftereffect.

Choose one main camera relationship, such as following the attacker, tracking a weapon, holding the contact point, following the receiver of force, observing with a long lens, or switching to POV. Change that relationship only for a meaningful peak. Do not use camera shake as a substitute for impact or fire effects without a trigger.

## Promotional or world-building PV

Give the audience a question, answer it progressively, then end with a larger question or a strong visible statement. Scale comes from changing rules, history, relationships, or spatial scope, not from generic cold-blue UI or giant titles.

Choose or derive an engine such as:

- local anomaly → environmental response → city/world effect → glimpse of truth;
- small relic → texture or sound activation → spatial memory → civilization reveal;
- character detail → architecture → geography → world rule;
- familiar space contradicts itself → viewpoint changes → hidden layer appears → new rule holds;
- several actions or symbols converge → threshold → spatial reorganization → payoff;
- present object → traces respond → past and present match in shape → conflict appears;
- character choice → environment or others respond → identity gains meaning → memory image.

Every shot must advance at least one of: scale, rule information, identity, historical trace, crisis or promise, or the state of the recurring image-derived anchor. Avoid empty atmosphere shots, generic destiny language, footstep-then-look-back openings, unrelated world-building symbols, and invented logos.

## High-budget flash edit

Fast cutting must follow a traceable motion or visual rule. Each cut delivers new information and catches the viewer's attention with a shared interface.

Select one primary flash engine and optionally one secondary engine:

1. Action sampling: sample one continuous action at different shot sizes and angles.
2. Editorial typography: compose the character, negative space, short text, and graphics as one system.
3. Shape-match chain: transform an image-derived shape across eyes, props, environment, light, or framing.
4. Spatial folding: reorganize one space through occlusion, reflection, rotation, page turns, or perspective shifts.
5. Identity slices: reveal clothing, prop, expression, action result, and environmental relation instead of a fixed eye-hand-full-body order.
6. Mixed-medium continuity: transform layers through one recurring image-derived object or shape.
7. Rhythm contrast: insert a 0.2–0.5-second overshoot, silence, residual inertia, or composition lock before the next release.

Adjacent shots share at least one interface: direction, shape, action state, prop, color block, text path, sound bridge, or occluding object. Across any three consecutive transitions, use at least two interface types. A constant abstract direction does not count toward this rotation rule. Avoid using the same concrete prop as the main interface for most cuts unless it is clearly the image's core identity or story anchor and changes state or meaning each time.

Choose one dominant camera relationship and at least two speed levels. Reuse 1–3 abstract connection logics with variation. Glitch, RGB splitting, white flashes, frame dropping, and 360-degree rotations are optional, not defaults.

When typography is appropriate, derive its colors, shapes, motion, and placement from the image's negative space, clothing details, props, or existing text. Keep text short, large, readable, and animated as part of the same motion system. Use 1–2 recurring text behaviors; do not create static sticker text, random UI, or unreadable layers.

## Concept differentiation gate

Before choosing the final direction, create three internal candidates:

- image-native: maximizes visible pose, prop, and spatial opportunities;
- concept-transformation: turns a visible object or relation into a rule, secret, or metaphor;
- formal-experiment: proposes a distinctive but executable camera, editing, graphic, or time structure.

Any two candidates must differ in at least four of these six dimensions: opening hook, narrative geometry, camera spine, transition logic, music topology, and ending payoff. Reject candidates that merely change characters, colors, or props while keeping the same shot order.

Do not use these as defaults unless supported by the image or user request: extreme eye close-up, hand toward camera, low-angle footsteps, 360-degree orbit, blue-black-white plus red danger accents, shattered glass, giant moon, petals, tunnels, cracks, RGB separation, glitch, white flashes, giant title sweeps, flirtatious face close-ups, or a fixed title-card ending.
